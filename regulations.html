Regulations and useful links

Fire safety law can feel like a maze. Here are the rules that matter, in plain English, plus the official sites that explain them.

Last updated 4 October 2026.

Laws

  • Regulatory Reform (Fire Safety) Order 2005

    The main fire law for businesses, workplaces and the shared areas of flats and HMOs. The responsible person must do a fire risk assessment and keep fire safety measures working.

  • Fire Safety Act 2021

    Makes it clear the Fire Safety Order also covers a block of flats’ structure, outside walls and flat front doors.

  • Fire Safety (England) Regulations 2022

    Extra duties for blocks of flats: fire safety instructions for residents, fire door checks in buildings over 11 m, and more for high-rise.

  • Building Safety Act 2022, section 156

    Since 1 October 2023, every business must record its full fire risk assessment in writing and share fire safety information with residents and other responsible persons.

  • Fire Safety (Residential Evacuation Plans) (England) Regulations 2025

    New from 6 April 2026. In blocks of flats 18 m+ (or over 11 m with a full-evacuation strategy), residents who would struggle to escape must be offered an evacuation plan.

  • Housing Act 2004

    Gives councils HMO licensing powers and the Housing Health and Safety Rating System (HHSRS) to judge hazards in homes, including fire.

  • Licensing of HMOs (Prescribed Description) (England) Order 2018

    An HMO with 5 or more people from 2 or more households needs a council licence, however many storeys it has.

  • Management of HMOs (England) Regulations 2006

    HMO managers must keep escape routes clear and keep fire alarms and firefighting equipment in good working order.

  • Smoke and Carbon Monoxide Alarm (England) Regulations 2015 (amended 2022)

    Rented homes need a smoke alarm on every storey and a CO alarm in any room with a fixed fuel-burning appliance (not gas cookers). Faulty alarms must be fixed once reported.

  • Renters’ Rights Act 2025

    Main changes from 1 May 2026: no more section 21 evictions, rolling tenancies, and tougher council fines for landlords who break the rules.

British Standards

The codes of practice the fire industry works to. The BSI pages are where to buy the full text.

  • BS 5839-1:2025 – fire alarms in non-domestic buildings

    Design, installation and servicing of fire alarms in businesses. New edition from April 2025, replacing the 2017 version.

  • BS 5839-6:2019+A1:2020 – fire alarms in homes

    Sets the right grade and level of alarm for houses, flats and HMOs.

  • BS 5306-3:2017 – fire extinguisher servicing

    Extinguishers need a service every year, plus an extended service or overhaul at set intervals depending on type.

  • BS 5266-1:2025 – emergency lighting

    Design, testing and upkeep of emergency lighting. New edition from October 2025, replacing the 2016 version.

  • PAS 79-1:2020 – fire risk assessment (non-housing)

    A step-by-step method for fire risk assessments in shops, offices, pubs and other non-housing premises.

  • BS 9792:2025 – fire risk assessment (housing)

    The method for fire risk assessments in HMOs, blocks of flats and student housing. Replaced PAS 79-2 in 2025.

Landlords and HMOs

  • LACORS Housing – Fire Safety guidance

    The guide councils use to decide what fire alarms, fire doors and escape routes a rented home or HMO needs.

  • House in multiple occupation licence (GOV.UK)

    Whether your HMO needs a licence and how to apply to your council.

  • HHSRS: landlord and agent guide (GOV.UK)

    How councils rate hazards such as fire in rented homes, and what they expect you to fix.

  • Smoke and carbon monoxide alarms: booklet for landlords (GOV.UK)

    Which alarms you need, where they go and when to test them.

  • Renters’ Rights Act: overview for landlords (GOV.UK)

    What has changed for landlords and what you need to do.

  • Fire safety in purpose-built blocks of flats (GOV.UK)

    Practical fire safety guidance for owners and managers of blocks of flats.

  • Fire Safety (England) Regulations 2022: your responsibilities (GOV.UK)

    A quick checklist of the 2022 duties by building height.

  • Residential PEEPs guidance (GOV.UK)

    Factsheet and guidance on the resident evacuation plan rules.

    Who is responsible for fire safety at work and what they must do.

  • Fire risk assessments (GOV.UK)

    What a fire risk assessment is and when to get help with one.

  • Fire safety risk assessment: 5-step checklist (GOV.UK)

    A simple checklist to work through your own assessment.

  • Making your small non-domestic premises safe from fire (GOV.UK)

    Short guide for small, simple shops, offices and workshops.

  • Fire safety: guidance for those with legal duties (GOV.UK)

  • Fire safety risk assessment: sleeping accommodation (GOV.UK)

    For hotels, hostels, guest houses and other places where people sleep.

    For B&Bs, holiday lets and Airbnb-style rooms.

  • Health and Safety Executive: fire safety

Local councils

Your council licenses HMOs and deals with unsafe rented homes.

  • Cambridge City Council – HMO licensing

    Apply for or renew an HMO licence and check the public register.

  • Cambridge City Council – private rented housing complaints

    Report poor conditions or safety problems in a rented home.

  • South Cambridgeshire District Council – HMO regulations

    HMO licensing, management rules and fees.

  • South Cambridgeshire District Council – rules for landlords

    The housing laws landlords must follow and how the council enforces them.

  • Huntingdonshire District Council – HMO licensing

    Apply online, plus HMO standards and fire safety guidance.

  • East Cambridgeshire District Council – HMO licensing

    Who needs a licence and how to apply.

  • Fenland District Council – HMO licensing

    Apply or renew, documents you need (including a fire alarm certificate) and the Wisbech HMO planning rules.

  • Peterborough City Council – HMOs

    HMO licensing and standards. Since 26 January 2026, smaller HMOs with 3 or 4 tenants need a licence too.

  • West Suffolk Council – HMO licence (Newmarket)

    HMO licensing for Newmarket and the rest of West Suffolk.

  • West Suffolk Council – private sector housing standards

    Advice and inspections for unsafe rented homes.

Fire service

  • Cambridgeshire Fire and Rescue – fire risk assessment and guidance

    Free business fire safety advice. Note: the fire service will not do your fire risk assessment for you.

  • Cambridgeshire Fire and Rescue – business safety service

    Fire safety audits and enforcement. Report a blocked or locked fire exit on 01480 444500.

  • Cambridgeshire Fire and Rescue – home fire safety

    Free online home safety check, and free home visits for vulnerable residents (0800 917 9994).

  • Suffolk Fire and Rescue – fire protection (Newmarket)

    Business and landlord fire safety advice, a free fire safety log book and false alarm tips.

Other help

  • National Fire Chiefs Council (NFCC)

    National fire safety guidance and campaigns from UK fire services.

  • Fire Industry Association (FIA) – fire safety advice

    Free guides on fire alarms, extinguishers and emergency lighting.

  • BAFE

    Independent register of third-party certificated fire safety companies, with guidance on choosing one.

  • National Residential Landlords Association (NRLA)

    Support, guides and training for private landlords.

  • Approved Document B – fire safety (GOV.UK)

    Building Regulations fire guidance for new builds, extensions and conversions, including HMO conversions.

  • LABC

    Find your local council building control team and building regulations advice.
